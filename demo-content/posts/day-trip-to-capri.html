Floating just off the tip of the Sorrentine peninsula, Capri has been a glamorous escape since the Roman emperors built their villas here. A day is enough to fall for it: dramatic sea stacks, a chic little piazzetta, and grottoes that glow an impossible blue.

Getting there

Fast ferries run to Capri from Sorrento, Positano, and Amalfi through the season. Book the first boat you reasonably can; the island fills quickly, and the light on the water is best in the morning. From the Marina Grande, a funicular climbs to Capri town and its famous piazzetta.

How to spend the day

  • Circle the island by boat — the only way to appreciate the Faraglioni, the sea caves, and the sheer scale of the cliffs.
  • Gardens of Augustus — manicured terraces with the postcard view of the Faraglioni and the winding Via Krupp.
  • Anacapri & Monte Solaro — ride the single-seat chairlift to the island's summit for a 360-degree panorama.
  • The Blue Grotto — weather permitting, row into the cave where the water turns luminous sapphire.

A word on prices

Capri is unapologetically expensive, especially the cafés on the piazzetta, where you pay handsomely for the people-watching. Keep costs down with a picnic from a Capri town deli, a swim from the rocks at Marina Piccola, and a gelato instead of a sit-down lunch.

Catch the late-afternoon ferry home and you will leave just as the day-trippers thin out and the island exhales — glowing, golden, and already pulling you back.
