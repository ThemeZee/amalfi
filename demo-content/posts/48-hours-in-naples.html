Most travellers rush through Naples on the way to the coast. Don't. Italy's most exuberant, chaotic, and soulful city rewards anyone who gives it a couple of days — and it happens to be the birthplace of pizza, so you will eat extraordinarily well while you are at it.

Day one: the historic heart

Lose yourself in the Centro Storico, a UNESCO-listed tangle of alleys where laundry flutters overhead and shrines glow in every corner. Walk Spaccanapoli, the dead-straight street that slices the old city in two, duck into the Cappella Sansevero to see the astonishing Veiled Christ, and let the street life carry you along.

Day two: views and treasures

  • National Archaeological Museum — the finest finds from Pompeii and Herculaneum, under one roof.
  • Certosa di San Martino — a hilltop monastery with the best panorama of the whole bay.
  • Lungomare — a seafront stroll past the Castel dell'Ovo at golden hour.

The pizza, of course

Neapolitan pizza is a protected craft and a point of civic pride: a blistered, chewy base, San Marzano tomatoes, and buffalo mozzarella, fired for ninety seconds in a screaming-hot oven. Order a classic Margherita or Marinara, expect a queue at the famous places, and know that there is barely a bad pizza in the whole city.

Keep your wits about you in the crowds, go with the city's wild energy rather than against it, and Naples will give you the richest 48 hours of your whole trip.
