An easy train ride from Naples and Sorrento lies one of the most extraordinary sites on earth: Pompeii, the Roman city frozen in time by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in AD 79. Walk its stone streets and the ancient world stops being a chapter in a textbook and becomes startlingly, almost unbearably, real.

What you will see

  • The Forum — the civic heart of the city, with Vesuvius looming behind it exactly as it did two thousand years ago.
  • Villa of the Mysteries — astonishing frescoes in deep Pompeian red, still vivid after twenty centuries.
  • The baths and bakeries — ovens, millstones, and mosaics that make daily Roman life feel immediate.
  • The plaster casts — haunting impressions of the city's final moments.

Climbing Vesuvius

With energy to spare, pair the ruins with the volcano that made them. A shuttle climbs most of the way up Vesuvius, leaving a stony 30-minute walk to the crater rim, where you can peer into the caldera and look out over the whole Bay of Naples.

Go early, wear a hat, and bring water. Pompeii is vast, almost entirely unshaded, and far bigger than first-timers expect.

Making it easy

The Circumvesuviana train links Naples and Sorrento with the Pompei Scavi stop, right by the entrance. Hire a guide or download an audio tour before you arrive — without one, the ruins are beautiful but silent, and so much of the wonder is in the stories.
