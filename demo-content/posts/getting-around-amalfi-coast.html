The Amalfi Coast is spectacular to travel through and, occasionally, maddening. One narrow road threads all the villages together, the buses are full, and the ferries answer to the weather. Understand your options, though, and getting around becomes part of the adventure rather than a source of stress.

By ferry — the best way in summer

From roughly April to October, ferries link Sorrento, Positano, Amalfi, Salerno, and the islands. They are faster than the bus, gloriously scenic, and they skip the traffic entirely. The one catch: rough seas can cancel sailings, so never pin a tight connection on the last boat of the day.

By bus — the scenic workhorse

The blue SITA buses run the coastal road all year and reach the hill towns the ferries cannot. Buy tickets before boarding from a tabacchi or newsstand, validate on board, and sit on the right-hand side heading from Sorrento to Amalfi for the finest views. In high season, queue early — buses fill fast.

Should you drive?

  • The coastal road is narrow, winding, and busy, with scarce and costly parking.
  • A scooter is nimble but only for confident riders.
  • A private driver costs more but removes every headache — worth it for a special day.

The golden rule of Amalfi transport: leave a buffer. The views are worth the wait, and the schedules keep their own relaxed time.

Mix and match — ferry out, bus back, a boat trip in between — and you will see the coast from every angle it deserves.
