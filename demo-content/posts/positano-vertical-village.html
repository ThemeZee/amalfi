No village on the coast announces itself quite like Positano. Approach by sea and it rises in front of you like an amphitheatre of colour: ochre, coral, and faded pink houses stacked so tightly up the cliff that the whole town seems to lean over the water to get a better look at you.

A town built on stairs

There is really only one road in Positano, and it loops down through the town in a long, lazy spiral. Everything else is steps — hundreds of them, threading between boutiques, bougainvillea, and tiny terraces. It sounds exhausting, and it is, but it is also the secret of the place. Every staircase opens onto a new view, and you will happily get lost for an afternoon.

What not to miss

  • Spiaggia Grande — the main beach, lined with fishing boats and restaurants, and the best spot to watch the town glow at sunset.
  • Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta — the majolica-tiled dome that crowns every photograph of Positano.
  • Fornillo Beach — a quieter cove a short walk west, away from the crowds.
  • The boutiques — Positano gave the world a breezy style all its own. Linen, sandals, and sunhats abound.

When to visit

Positano is busiest from mid-morning, when the day-trippers arrive, until late afternoon, when they leave. Stay the night if you can; the town is at its most magical in the early morning and after dark, when the lamps come on and the stairways empty. If you are visiting for the day, arrive early or come by evening ferry.
