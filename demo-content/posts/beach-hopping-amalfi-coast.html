The Amalfi Coast is not a place of long golden sands. Its beaches are small, pebbly, and tucked between cliffs — and all the more special for it. Some you reach by a flight of steps, others only by boat, and the best way to find your favourite is simply to go hopping from one cove to the next.

Beaches worth the steps

  • Spiaggia Grande, Positano — the lively main beach, with sunbeds, boats, and that unbeatable backdrop.
  • Fornillo, Positano — quieter and softer, a ten-minute walk around the headland.
  • Marina di Praia — a dramatic sliver of beach wedged in a cliff gorge near Praiano.
  • Atrani — a small, friendly town beach with a real local feel.

The secret coves

The coast's finest swimming spots have no road at all. Hire a small boat for a half-day, or hop on a shared water taxi, and you can drop anchor in hidden bays beneath the cliffs — the Fiordo di Furore, the rocks below Conca dei Marini — where the water is clear and the only sound is the sea against the stone.

Beach-club or free?

Most beaches mix a paid section — loungers, umbrellas, a bar — with a free spiaggia libera at one end. A sunbed can cost as much as a good lunch in peak season, so bring a towel if you would rather keep it simple. Water shoes are a wise buy for the pebbles.

Swim last thing before the boats come back, when the light goes soft and the coves empty. That is the Amalfi Coast at its most perfect.
