The Amalfi Coast is one of those places that looks almost unreal in photographs and then, somehow, exceeds them in person. Fifty kilometres of cliffs pleated with pastel villages, lemon groves clinging to impossible slopes, and a sea that shifts from turquoise to deep indigo as the afternoon wears on. If this is your first trip, here is everything we wish we had known before our own.

When to go

The coast has a short, intense high season. July and August bring the warmest water and the largest crowds, with hotel prices to match. For the best balance, aim for late May, June, or September: the sea is warm enough to swim, the light is golden, and the coastal road is far less frantic. April and October are quieter and cheaper still, though some beach clubs and ferries wind down.

Where to base yourself

  • Positano — the postcard. Glamorous, steep, and unforgettable, but you will climb a lot of stairs.
  • Amalfi & Atrani — central and well connected by ferry, with a grand cathedral and a quieter neighbour next door.
  • Ravello — high above the coast, calm and romantic, famous for its gardens and summer concerts.
  • Sorrento — technically just off the coast, but flat, affordable, and the easiest transport hub for day trips.

Getting here

The nearest airport is Naples. From there, the most relaxing route in summer is the ferry via Sorrento; the most scenic is the SITA bus along the coastal road; and the most flexible (though not always fastest) is a private transfer. We cover the details in our dedicated guide to getting around, but the short version is: do not plan to drive unless you truly love hairpin bends.

Slow down. The Amalfi Coast rewards travellers who linger over a long lunch far more than those racing to tick off every village.

A few first-timer tips

  • Pack flat, grippy shoes. The charm of these villages is also several hundred steps.
  • Carry some cash for small cafés, beach loungers, and the occasional bus ticket.
  • Book restaurants with a view a day ahead in high season.
  • Build in a buffer day. Ferries and buses run on their own gentle sense of time.

Do all that, and the coast will do the rest. Benvenuti sulla Costiera Amalfitana.
