Few flavours capture a place the way limoncello captures the Amalfi Coast. Bright, cold, and almost shockingly lemony, it is the drink that ends every meal here — and the scent of its fruit, the giant knobbly sfusato amalfitano, hangs over the whole coastline from the terraced groves above the sea.

The lemon that built a coast

Amalfi lemons are enormous, thick-skinned, and remarkably sweet, grown on stone terraces that have climbed these slopes for a thousand years. The groves are a protected landscape, tended by hand on ground too steep for any machine. That fragrant rind — not the juice — is the secret of a good limoncello.

How it is made

  • Peel the lemons thinly, taking only the yellow zest and none of the bitter white pith.
  • Steep the peel in a neutral spirit for a week or more, until the alcohol turns deep gold.
  • Stir in a simple syrup of sugar and water, then rest and filter.
  • Serve it ice-cold from the freezer, in a small chilled glass.

Every family on the coast swears their recipe is the best — and after a few tastings, you will happily let them keep arguing.

Beyond the glass

The lemons turn up everywhere: in delizia al limone, the coast's beloved lemon-cream dome of a cake; in sorbet served inside the hollowed fruit; and shaved raw into salads with mint and good oil. Take a jar of lemon marmalade home and you can keep a little of the Amalfi sunlight on your breakfast table all winter.
