On the Amalfi Coast, lunch is a landscape. The catch comes in each morning, the lemons grow on the hillside above the kitchen, and the best tables sit so close to the water you could almost rinse your hands in the sea. Here is how to eat well, from harbour shacks to white-tablecloth terraces.

The dishes to order

  • Spaghetti alle vongole — clams, garlic, white wine, and good olive oil. Simplicity perfected.
  • Scialatielli ai frutti di mare — fresh handmade pasta tangled with the morning's mixed catch.
  • Pesce all'acqua pazza — whole fish poached in "crazy water" with tomato and herbs.
  • Fritto misto — a paper cone of lightly fried anchovies, calamari, and prawns, eaten by the harbour.

Where to find it

The grand terrace restaurants in Positano and Ravello are worth it once, for the view as much as the food. But some of our best meals have been in tiny family trattorias in Atrani, Cetara, and the back lanes of Amalfi, where the menu is short, the fish is whatever came in, and the owner's mother is still in the kitchen.

A local secret: Cetara

This little fishing village is the anchovy capital of the coast and the home of colatura di alici, a golden anchovy essence descended from the Roman garum. Order spaghetti dressed with it and you are tasting two thousand years of coastal cooking in a single forkful.

Follow one rule and you will rarely go wrong: eat where the menu is shortest and the locals are loudest.

Finish, always, with a chilled glass of limoncello the house will bring unbidden — and a long, unhurried look at the sea.
